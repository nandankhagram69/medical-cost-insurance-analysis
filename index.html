📄 Project Proposal & Research Summary

Alignment with the Course Proposal (IU2441230558, IU2441230559 - PSC CE0525, 5 CSE-F)

1. Proposal Scope & Objectives

  • Project Definition: Analysis of medical insurance charges using Python, exploring age, sex, BMI, number of children, smoking status, and region.
  • Dataset: Kaggle Medical Cost Personal Datasets (1,338 rows, 7 attributes).
  • Core Deliverables: Data cleaning, EDA, 6 statistical graphs, interactive user interface, and an insights report.
  • Tools: Python, Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn, JavaScript/HTML5 UI, Tkinter GUI.
  • GitHub Repository: nandankhagram69/medical-cost-insurance-analysis

2. Data Cleaning Summary

  • Missing Values: Verified 0 missing/null entries across all 7 columns.
  • Duplicates: 1 duplicate record identified and preserved/documented per standard data cleaning protocols.
  • Data Types: Clean mapping of numeric (`age`, `bmi`, `children`, `charges`) and categorical (`sex`, `smoker`, `region`).
  • Outlier Checks: High charges were confirmed as legitimate high-risk smoker and extreme BMI cases rather than data collection errors.

3. Empirical Findings & Report

Smoker Status Discrepancy

Smoking accounts for the largest variance in insurance premiums. The regression coefficient for smokers is +$23,848.53, making smoking status the overwhelming risk predictor.

Obesity Combined Effect

Non-smokers with high BMI see gradual cost increases. However, individuals who are both smokers and clinically obese (BMI ≥ 30) suffer a compound cost penalty, routinely facing charges above $35,000–$45,000.

Regional Differences

Southeast charges average $14,735, highest among all 4 regions, due to higher obesity rates and smoking prevalence in that geographical sample.

Model Performance

The Multiple Linear Regression model achieves an $R^2$ score of 0.751, proving that 75.1% of cost variability can be explained by these 6 demographic and health metrics.
